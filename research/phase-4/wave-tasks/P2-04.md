## The finding (lead-verified from disk)

Pie fails its hover gate with a **fixed, deterministic** delta — four runs across four days, stable to three decimal places:

| run | settled | hover-30 | hover-50 | hover-70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `qa/results/pie/2026-08-19T20-50-25-175Z` | 0.0083% PASS | **3.1827% FAIL** | **0.0000% PASS** | **3.1716% FAIL** |
| `…/2026-08-21T11-12-03-805Z` | 0.0085% PASS | **3.1828% FAIL** | **0.0000% PASS** | **3.1712% FAIL** |
| `…/2026-08-22T11-24-33-806Z` | 0.0124% PASS | **3.1829% FAIL** | **0.0000% PASS** | **3.1714% FAIL** |
| `…/2026-08-22T11-31-04-763Z` | 0.0139% PASS | **3.1842% FAIL** | **0.0000% PASS** | **3.1721% FAIL** |

All at `--n 1000`. Gate is 0.5%.

**Read the shape of that table before you start — it is the whole lead.** `settled` is essentially clean, so the two impls draw the same pie. `hover-50` is **exactly 0.0000%**, so at that probe point they are pixel-identical. Only hover-30 and hover-70 diverge, and by a large, near-constant ~3.18%.

That is a **probe-position-specific hover-state divergence**, not a diffuse rendering drift: at hover-50 the probe almost certainly hovers nothing (dead centre / outside the ring), and the two impls agree perfectly; at 30 and 70 a slice **is** hovered and the two render that hovered state differently. ~3.18% of a 1200×800 viewport is a large area — consistent with a whole slice being drawn in a different position/size (a hover "explode" offset divergence), or a tooltip/overlay panel, rather than an antialiasing seam.

Your first job is to confirm or refute that reading, then fix the cause.

## Where to look

Start by inspecting the existing diff PNGs — they will tell you immediately whether the differing pixels sit on a slice edge, on a whole displaced slice, or in a tooltip panel:
```
qa/results/pie/2026-08-22T11-31-04-763Z/    # hover-30 / hover-70 diff PNGs
```

Then the two leading suspects, both already documented in `research/phase-4/synthesis/parity.md`:

- **P3 — `DEFAULT_HOVER_OFFSET`** (`parity.md:106`): legacy barrel-exports it; migrated has it module-local. The finding is scoped as an export-surface gap ("FIX — one-line barrel add"), **but** check whether the *value* or its *application* also diverges. A hover-offset difference is exactly what would displace a whole slice by a few percent of the viewport.
- **P8+P10** (`parity.md:111`): legacy uses `mouseenter`/`mouseleave` and cn/grid stacking; migrated uses **pointer events** and inline styles / an absolute overlay. Ruled `ACCEPT — global event-model ratify + disclosed styling`.

### On the ruled ACCEPTs — important
P8/P10 and P1/P7/P9 are **ruled ACCEPT**. You may not reopen a ruling on your own. But a ruling accepts a *described* divergence; it does not license an unbounded pixel delta that nobody measured at the time. If your evidence shows the accepted styling approach (absolute overlay / inline styles) is what produces the 3.18%, that is a genuine finding: **report it and STOP rather than reverting a ruled decision.** The lead will re-rule.

Conversely, if the cause is an ordinary bug — a wrong offset, a mis-centred transform, a missing clamp — just fix it.

### Two harness gotchas that will waste your time
1. **`pnpm qa -- --chart x` is broken** — it injects a literal `"--"` argv element the parser rejects. Invoke `node qa/screenshot.mjs` directly, as above.
2. **Run pie solo, never in a batch.** Concurrent/batched QA runs contend and manufacture false pixel failures — this has already produced two spurious "regressions" in this project. Standing rule: **a batch-mode FAIL is not a finding until a solo repro confirms it.** Batch mode also shares ONE `--n` across the batch, which is wrong for pie.
